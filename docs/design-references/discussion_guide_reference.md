## 01 &nbsp;&nbsp; Introduction and consent

*5 minutes. Read warmly, not robotically. Confirm consent before proceeding.*

#### Welcome

> Hi [participant], thank you for taking the time to talk with me today. I'm Lapedra, and I'm part of a team studying how Veterans use the VA mobile app. Your perspective will directly help shape improvements that affect thousands of users like you.

#### Session purpose

> Today we'll look at how you navigate through the VA mobile app to complete some common tasks. I'm interested in understanding what feels intuitive and what feels confusing. I'm not testing you — I'm testing the app. There are no wrong answers.

#### Consent and recording

> I'd like to record our session so I can focus on our conversation rather than taking notes. The recording is only used by our research team and won't be shared outside it. Your name and personal information will be kept confidential. Is it okay if I record?

#### Ground rules

> A few things to keep in mind: there are no right or wrong answers. Please think out loud as much as you can — tell me what you're noticing, even if it seems obvious. If any question makes you uncomfortable, just let me know and we'll skip it. Take a break anytime.

## 02 &nbsp;&nbsp; Warm-up

*8 minutes. Build rapport. Surface context that will inform later observations.*

#### Questions

1. Tell me a bit about your military service and how long you've been using VA services.
   - *Why this matters:* Establishes rapport, surfaces VA familiarity

2. How do you typically manage your VA healthcare and benefits — website, mobile app, phone, or in-person?
   - *Why this matters:* Reveals current channel preferences before focusing on mobile

3. When you use apps on your phone, what makes one easy or frustrating to use?
   - *Why this matters:* Surfaces general usability expectations and accessibility needs without leading

4. Think about the last time you used the VA mobile app — what were you trying to do?
   - *Why this matters:* Primes them to think about the app and reveals real-world usage

#### If participant uses assistive technology

Ask them to describe their setup before screen sharing begins:

- Which assistive technology do you use most often?
- Any settings I should know about (text size, contrast, voice control)?

This frames later observations and lets you check that the screen sharing setup will capture what you need.

## 03 &nbsp;&nbsp; Tasks

*40 minutes total. For each task: read the scenario verbatim, watch without helping unless completely stuck, capture observations.*

### Task 01 &nbsp;·&nbsp; 8 min &nbsp;·&nbsp; Prescription refill

#### Scenario

> You're at home and realize you're running low on a regular medication. You want to request a refill using the VA mobile app.

#### Instruction

> Show me how you would request a prescription refill. Please think aloud as you navigate.

#### Success criteria

- Reaches prescription refill within 2 taps from home screen
- Understands how to initiate the refill process

#### Observe

- First tap location and stated reasoning
- Hesitation or backtracking at navigation points
- Whether they explore Health section first or try other areas
- Search usage if they get stuck

#### Probe

- "What did you expect to find when you tapped [section]?"
- "On a scale of 1–5, how easy was that to find?"

---

### Task 02 &nbsp;·&nbsp; 8 min &nbsp;·&nbsp; Section differentiation

#### Scenario

> I'll give you a few things you might want to do with the VA app. For each one, tell me where you'd expect to find it without actually tapping.

#### Instruction

> Just point to where you think each of these would be located:
> 1. Update your mailing address
> 2. View your disability rating
> 3. Check your next appointment
> 4. Download a benefits letter

#### Success criteria

- Correctly identifies Health vs Benefits vs Profile sections
- Demonstrates understanding of information architecture logic

#### Observe

- Confidence level in predictions (hesitant vs. immediate)
- Confusion between Benefits and Profile
- Whether mental model matches actual organization

#### Probe

- "What's the difference between Benefits and Profile in your mind?"
- "Where would you go if you weren't sure?"

---

### Task 03 &nbsp;·&nbsp; 8 min &nbsp;·&nbsp; Appointment discovery

#### Scenario

> You have a doctor's appointment coming up but can't remember exactly when it is. You need to check the details.

#### Instruction

> Show me how you would find information about your upcoming appointments.

#### Success criteria

- Navigates to appointments section
- Locates upcoming appointment details

#### Observe

- Initial navigation choice and path taken
- Whether they distinguish between past and upcoming appointments
- Interaction with appointment details

#### Probe

- "Is this where you expected to find appointments?"
- "What other information would be helpful to see here?"

---

### Task 04 &nbsp;·&nbsp; 8 min &nbsp;·&nbsp; Recovery behavior

#### Scenario

> Let's say you're looking for information about your GI Bill benefits, but you're not sure where to find it in the app.

#### Instruction

> Show me what you would do if you couldn't immediately find what you were looking for.

#### Success criteria

- Demonstrates a recovery strategy (search, exploration, or exit behavior)
- Shows problem-solving approach

#### Observe

- Whether they use search functionality
- Exploration patterns when lost
- Point at which they might abandon the task

#### Probe

- "What would you do next if this didn't work?"
- "Have you ever called VA support instead of using the app?"

---

### Task 05 &nbsp;·&nbsp; 8 min &nbsp;·&nbsp; Assistive technology navigation

*Skip this task if participant does not use assistive technology. Replace with extended exploration of one prior task.*

#### Scenario

> Now I'd like you to navigate using your usual assistive technology as you normally would.

#### Instruction

> Using your screen reader (or other AT), show me how you would get to the Benefits section and explore what's available there.

#### Success criteria

- Successfully navigates bottom navigation with assistive technology
- Can identify and access Benefits section content

#### Observe

- Screen reader announcement clarity for navigation items
- Swipe patterns and navigation efficiency
- Frustration points with focus order or labeling

#### Probe

- "How clear were those navigation labels?"
- "Did the screen reader announce what you expected?"

## 04 &nbsp;&nbsp; Retrospective

*5 minutes. Surface overall impressions, frustrations, unmet needs.*

#### Questions

1. **Overall impressions** — What's your overall impression of navigating the app today?
2. **Pain points** — What was the most frustrating or confusing part?
3. **Mental model** — How do you think about the difference between Health, Benefits, and Profile sections?
4. **Improvements** — If you could change one thing about how the app is organized, what would it be?
5. **Likelihood to use** — On a scale of 1–10, how likely would you be to use this app regularly? What would make it a 10?
6. **Anything else** — Is there anything else you'd like to share that I didn't ask about?

2. What surprised me about this participant's experience?
3. What needs deeper investigation in remaining sessions?

6. How confident am I in these findings? What's still unclear?

## Emergency procedures

#### Technical issues

- Backup meeting link ready before session starts
- Screen share troubleshooting: check browser permissions, try a different browser, fall back to phone
- Have phone number as backup contact method

#### Participant distress

- Pause session immediately and check comfort level
- Remind them they can stop at any time and there are no wrong answers
- For Veterans: VA Crisis Line — call 988, then press 1 (24/7 support)

#### Data and privacy concerns

- Reassure about data handling and anonymization
- Review consent form if questions arise
- Stop recording immediately if requested
