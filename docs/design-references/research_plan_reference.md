## Summary

This study will identify the navigation pathways and information architecture decisions in the VA Health & Benefits Mobile App that cause Veterans to abandon tasks. Through 8–10 moderated usability sessions with Veterans across diverse accessibility profiles, the research will surface specific friction points, document mental model mismatches, and produce evidence-based recommendations for the Q3 2026 redesign sprint.

## Background

The VA Health & Benefits Mobile App's current navigation produces a 45% task abandonment rate and a 4.2/10 user satisfaction rating. This research will determine how Veterans navigate the app to complete critical tasks, where they encounter barriers, and what information architecture changes will reduce abandonment. Findings will inform the Q3 2026 mobile app redesign sprint, with specific focus on decisions about progressive disclosure, dashboard layout, and feature discoverability.

---

## Objectives

What we aim to learn through this research:

- **Identify** specific navigation pathways and information architecture elements that cause task abandonment
- **Understand** how Veterans mentally categorize health and benefits information, and where current categorization conflicts with their expectations
- **Evaluate** which existing navigation patterns enable confident task completion versus those that create barriers
- **Document** accessibility barriers experienced by Veterans using assistive technologies

---

## Research questions

1. At what specific points do Veterans abandon tasks or experience confusion in the current navigation?
2. How do Veterans' mental models for organizing health and benefits information differ from the current app structure?
3. Which navigation patterns successfully enable task completion versus those that create barriers?
4. What accessibility improvements are needed to support Veterans using assistive technologies?

---

## Method

**Approach** — Moderated remote usability sessions combining think-aloud protocol with task-based scenarios. Sessions will explore navigation pathways for high-frequency tasks (appointment scheduling, prescription refills, secure messaging, benefits lookup). Post-task interviews will surface mental models and contextual factors not visible during task completion.

**Session format** — 90-minute remote sessions via video conferencing with screen sharing. Participants using assistive technology will use their own devices and configurations to ensure realistic conditions.

**Data collection** — Think-aloud protocol, screen recording, observer notes, post-session structured interviews

**Tasks** — Realistic scenarios drawn from the most-used app functions: schedule appointment, refill prescription, send secure message, locate benefit information, update contact information

Detailed task scripts and discussion guide will be developed separately and reviewed before fielding.

---

## Participants

**Sample size** — 8–10 Veterans

**Required composition:**

- All currently use or recently attempted to use the VA Health & Benefits Mobile App
- Mixed device types (iOS and Android, roughly proportional to current user base)
- At least 50% using assistive technology (screen readers, large text, voice control)
- Range of usage frequency (daily, weekly, monthly)
- Range of service eras (Post-9/11, Gulf War, Vietnam-era)

**Recruitment** — Internal VA panel, partnerships with disability services offices, Veteran service organizations. Backup recruitment through assistive technology support groups if primary channels do not yield sufficient diversity.

**Compensation** — $75 per participant via Amazon gift card or equivalent, distributed within 24 hours of session completion.
