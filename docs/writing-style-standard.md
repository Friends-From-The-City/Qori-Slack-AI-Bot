# Qori Generated-Document Writing Style Standard

**Status:** Ratified v1.0 (2026-08-05). New template work conforms immediately; enforcement pass post-launch.

---

## 1. Voice — Who is speaking

Qori documents speak in **the researcher's analytical voice**, not Qori's. The "Generated by Qori" footer is the only place Qori identifies itself as a tool. Everywhere else, the document reads as if a skilled analyst wrote it on the researcher's behalf.

**In practice:** No "Qori found that..." or "The system identified..." The document makes claims directly: "The calendar interface is inaccessible to screen reader users." Not: "Qori's analysis suggests the calendar interface may present accessibility challenges."

**Boundary:** The warm second-person voice ("Nice work, Lapedra!") belongs in Slack notifications. Documents are permanent artifacts shared with stakeholders — they use the analytical third-person voice.

---

## 2. Person — Conventions by document type

| Document type | Person | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Findings (readout, session summary, synthesis) | Third person | "PT-001 abandoned the task." "Veterans with AT are excluded." |
| Planning (brief, plan, discussion guide) | Third person | "This study will recruit 8 veterans." The brief is an artifact a stakeholder approves; "this study will" makes the document the commitment-bearer, matching the approval workflow. Never "we." |
| Communication (outreach) | Second person (researcher→participant) | "We'd like to invite you to participate..." |

**Ruling (2026-08-05):** Third person locked for planning documents. "This study will" — never "we."

---

## 3. Tense

| Content type | Tense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Findings — observed behavior | Simple past | "PT-001 abandoned the task three times." |
| Findings — current state | Present | "The calendar interface is inaccessible." |
| Quotes | As spoken — no change | Reproduce verbatim |
| Recommendations | Present imperative / modal | "Implement accessible controls." / "The team should audit..." |
| Planning | Future | "This study will recruit 8 veterans." |
| Methodology | Present (standing practice) | "Think-aloud protocol captures reasoning as it happens." |
| Background | Present for conditions, past for events | "The app currently shows 45% abandonment." / "The redesign shipped in Q1." |

**Rule of thumb:** Findings describe a world that exists now (present), arrived at through events (past). Planning describes a world being created (future).

## 4. R5 jargon control

**Never in document output:**

| System term | Translation |
|---|---|
| cascade, emit, consume, nugget, pool | No equivalent — the document shows the result, not the pipeline |
| atomic_nugget, nugget-PT001-003 | Use participant IDs (PT-001) and section references |
| yamlProcessor, handler, upstream_ | Never appears |
| TemplateContractError | "Required data is missing" in user-facing messages |

**Acceptable in appendices only:**

| Term | Where |
|---|---|
| TB-XXX, RQ-XXX, OBJ-XXX | Upstream Context, Barrier Validation, Brief Commitments — cross-reference IDs, not jargon |
| nugget-PT-XXX-NNN | Evidence chain lines in appendix toggles only |
| "Generated by Qori" | Footer only |

**Rule:** If it would confuse a VA product manager reading the body, it doesn't belong there.

## 5. Findings phrasing

**Pattern: Participant-as-subject, observed behavior, concrete impact.**

- "PT-001 attempted to schedule an appointment three times before abandoning the task."
- "Two of three participants could not complete appointment scheduling independently."
- "The calendar announces 'button' repeatedly instead of dates, making date selection impossible for screen reader users."

**Wrong:** "There were accessibility issues" (vague). "Users may experience difficulty" (hedged). "The calendar could be improved" (recommendation as finding).

**IDs:** PT-XXX for individual quotes/actions. "Participants" (plural, no ID) for cross-participant patterns. "X of Y participants" for proportions. Never real names.

**Confidence in prose:** The confidence level is in the metadata line, not restated in prose. Don't write "It appears that..." in a Strong finding. Reserve hedging for Limited confidence where the prose should convey inferential distance.

**Suggested owners:** Generic role categories only ("frontend engineering," "content design," "accessibility team"). Never named individuals or invented team names ("Policy Team," "Innovation Lab"). If the upstream data names a specific team, use it; otherwise default to generic roles.

**Quote integrity conventions:**
- Quotes are verbatim. Never smoothed, corrected, or paraphrased inside quotation marks.
- Omissions are marked: "I tried three times [...] then I just called."
- Clarifying insertions use brackets: "It [the calendar] just says button, button, button."
- PII redactions show the replacement token where the removal occurred: "my doctor at [LOCATION] said..."
- If a quote requires so much bracketing that it no longer reads as the participant's words, paraphrase OUTSIDE quotation marks and attribute: PT-001 described trying three times before calling.

## 6. Uncertainty language

| Confidence | Prose treatment | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Strong** | Direct statement | "The calendar interface is inaccessible." |
| **Moderate** | Show the basis | "For PT-002, navigation labels disappeared — consistent with the large-text behavior in session 1." |
| **Limited** | Signal inferential distance | "PT-003 appeared to rely on call-in support when digital navigation failed, though not directly stated." |

**"May" vs. "does":**
- **"does"** when evidence is direct (quote, task failure, cross-participant pattern)
- **"may"** when finding rests on inference or a single data point
- Never "may" on Strong. Never "does" on Limited.

**Anti-patterns:**

| Wrong | Fix |
|---|---|
| "It seems likely veterans find navigation confusing." | "3 of 3 participants expressed confusion at the Health tab hierarchy." |
| "The app could potentially be improved by..." | "Implementing ARIA labels resolves the VoiceOver announcement failure." |
| "Research suggests that..." | State the finding directly — this isn't citing literature, it's reporting this study |

**Fabrication vs. uncertainty:** Anti-fabrication rules address inventing data. Uncertainty language applies when data exists but is ambiguous. If data does not exist, omit — don't hedge into existence.

## 7. Participant description language

Documents describe participants respectfully and functionally.

**Functional descriptions, not identity labels:**

| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| "the disabled," "disabled participants" | "participants using assistive technology" |
| "suffers from," "afflicted with" | "has," or state the functional context: "uses a screen reader" |
| "wheelchair-bound," "confined to" | "uses a wheelchair" |
| "impaired participants" | name the interaction: "screen reader users," "large-text users" |

**Rules:**
- Disability or health information appears ONLY when research-relevant to the finding, and only as explicitly stated by the participant (per the session_summary grounding rules — never inferred).
- Service details are stated as the participant framed them ("Vietnam-era veteran" if that is what they said), not elaborated.
- Age, race/ethnicity, and other demographics appear in documents only where the research question concerns them; the default is absence.

**Test:** Would a VA accessibility reviewer — or the participant themselves — read the description as accurate and respectful?

## Codified conventions (from enforcement review)

1. **Planning doc person:** Third person locked. "This study will" — never "we." (Ruled 2026-08-05)
2. **Confidence parenthetical:** Required in findings documents (parenthetical reasoning mandatory). Not applicable in planning documents. (Codified 2026-08-05)
3. **Suggested owners:** Generic role categories only — promoted to §5 standing rule. (Ruled 2026-08-05)
4. **Summary mixed tense:** Present for current state, past for session events. Intentional — follows §3 rule of thumb. (Codified 2026-08-05)
